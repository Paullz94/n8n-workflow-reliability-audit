Why this exists

Reliability is not the same as “the scenario ran”.

A workflow can complete without a red error and still duplicate a write, skip an important record, continue after a failed side effect or leave two systems out of sync.

01

Duplicate effects

Retries and repeated events can create the same external action more than once.

02

Silent skips

Filters and handlers can let the scenario continue while expected business work never happened.

03

Partial state

A later failure can leave earlier systems updated and downstream systems untouched.

04

Race conditions

Concurrent runs can overwrite or reorder state when the same business key is involved.

Not a generic score

Every important finding should answer four questions.

What did the blueprint show? Why could that matter? What should change? How do you safely verify the change?

Evidence
A write-like module has automatic retry behavior.
Risk
The first attempt may have committed even if its response looked like a failure.
Action
Use an idempotency key, upsert or destination-specific duplicate guard.
Verify
Replay the same synthetic event and confirm only one intended side effect exists.

Boundaries

What PCFlows does not pretend to prove.

A blueprint is structural evidence, not a live production system. A clean static result does not prove correct credentials, healthy third-party APIs, complete business logic, security compliance or the absence of defects.
